Mutual Futures / GAJRA Earth

What should guide the transition?

GAJRA Earth, the Global Association for Joyful Responsible Abundance on Earth, proposes a shared point of reflection for human self-alignment and AI alignment. Different people and cultures help define what a good future means.

Give the principle practical meaning

Joy includes belonging, curiosity, creativity, love, rest and the freedom to choose a life. Responsibility means attending to consequences for ourselves, other people, other species and those who come after us. Abundance means expanding access to the food, housing, energy, knowledge, care and productive capacity that make those lives possible.

These meanings are a starting point for discussion. A community may express them differently. The garland image behind GAJRA connects distinct flowers without making them identical.

Human self-alignment starts with experience

Self-alignment means examining whether our actions and circumstances match what matters to us. A person might value family time but find that work and care leave none. A community might value clean water but lack the resources to protect it. Reflection should reveal a change worth making.

Personal reflection can remain private. People choose which experiences, questions or priorities to share, and with whom.

AI alignment needs human evidence and correction

People’s experiences can help inform the objectives, evaluations and oversight of AI systems. Agreement on a slogan cannot settle every conflict. A system still needs a defined task, permission to act, evaluation in its setting and a way for people to challenge or stop it.

Joyful Responsible Abundance supplies questions for that work. It is a guiding principle, not a demonstration that an AI system is safe or aligned.

QuestionApply it to a workplace AI upgrade
Does it improve life?Ask workers whether the change reduces exhausting work and creates usable time or skills.
Who carries the consequences?Cost job changes, energy use, maintenance, mistakes and effects on customers before approving the change.
Who gains capability?Examine who owns the equipment, who can learn to use it and where the gains go.

Reflect, build, measure and revise

The proposed process runs from defining and reflecting to imagining, building, measuring, sharing and aligning again. Small projects give people something concrete to assess; shared learning lets other places adapt what works.

Record costs and effects alongside people’s accounts of their lives. Ask whose experience is missing. Use what is learned to revise the project and its assumptions, including the meaning of success.

Cooperate across places without erasing differences

Local groups, First Nations and wider institutions retain their own authority and choice of participation. Global cooperation can share research, skills, resources and lessons without requiring one culture, one AI provider or a central authority over private lives.

Mutual Futures explores the productive and social arrangements that could put this principle to work. Queens adds leadership and capital; C-Hour adds recognition of voluntary contribution.
