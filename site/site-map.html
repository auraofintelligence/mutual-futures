Mutual Futures.

Choose a topic that interests you. Each page explains one part of the idea, with links to related topics and sources.

On this page
    1. Joyful Responsible AbundanceAI and automation raise a human question: how do we turn growing capability into a better life for everyone? Mutual Futures proposes global change across ownership, income, learning, care and public decisions, guided by Joyful Responsible Abundance.
    2. GAJRA EarthGAJRA Earth, the Global Association for Joyful Responsible Abundance on Earth, proposes a shared point of reflection for human self-alignment and AI alignment. Different people and cultures help define what a good future means.
    3. Mutual wealthA mutual is an organisation owned for the benefit of its members. Here, the idea is to give people a clear say in shared businesses and a fair share of the benefits, with clear rules about costs, responsibilities and leaving.
    4. Business successionA retiring owner gains a fair exit and room for their next chapter. Younger generations learn from the knowledge built over a lifetime, while AI, robotics and extended reality (XR) help them advance the business and share in its future.
    5. Queens & leadership500 Queens proposes pathways for women to become enterprise leaders and decision-makers, backed by learning, mentors, productive assets and capital. Its connection to Mutual Futures is practical: who gets to lead the transition and decide how its benefits are used?
    6. Capital & financeBuying a business is only the start. There must also be money for wages, supplies, repairs and improvements. Explore how a purchase might be funded and try the calculator with your own assumptions.
    7. Work & lifeAI, robotics and extended reality (XR) could help people learn new work while established businesses improve their capabilities. Try Everything Once explores supported moves between roles; Intermittent Retirement explores planned periods for family, travel, learning and rest.
    8. Intelligence & incomeAutomation can expand what we produce. Whether that improves lives depends on access, ownership and how the gains are shared. Mutual Futures connects useful intelligence for everyone with the aim of an adequate income and time to choose how to live.
    9. Care & C-HoursCare, practical help, teaching and community work sustain daily life. C-Hour proposes a way to recognise voluntary human time, while Mutual Futures develops the income, services and time that let people choose how to contribute.
    10. Personal AI & privacyPersonal AI could help you understand information, reflect on experience and make choices. Mutual Futures connects that capability with GAJRA’s question of alignment: does the system help you act on what matters to you, under permissions you can understand?
    11. Legal reflectionLaws affect our work, homes and shared future. This part of the proposal asks how people could understand those rules, see their effects and explore changes through lawful public processes.
    12. Try a simulationIf the power goes out, water pumps, food storage and communications may be affected too. A simulation is a model for exploring connections like these before making changes in the real world.
    13. Health & careGood food, rest, care and a healthy environment belong in any plan for a better life. This proposal explores shared services and separate research projects, with people choosing how they take part.
    14. Preparing for disruptionReliable water, energy, food, shelter and practical skills help communities through difficult times. The wider ambition is to preserve those foundations, our knowledge and our choices for future generations.
    15. The long-term futureWhat could humanity build over many generations? This long-term vision connects practical work today with future habitats, space exploration, science, art and play. The most distant ideas remain speculative.
    16. World travelTravel can mean learning a skill, visiting family, joining a project or simply having a break. A shared work network could help people explore while keeping a connection to home.
    17. Oceania & Native NationsCommunities across Oceania and beyond could share useful ideas, skills and resources while making their own decisions. Each community and First Nation would choose whether to take part and on what terms.
    18. Culture & world stageMusic, film, art and gatherings help people imagine and discuss a different future. They are also worthwhile parts of life in their own right.
    19. Possible next stepsA community activity, a leadership opportunity, a useful technology trial or a business handover can each be a starting point. Connect the results to the wider transition in ownership, income, learning and public decisions.
    20. Take partHelp turn the ideas into work that people can assess. Start with one situation you understand and the people whose lives it could improve.
    21. Document libraryStart with the current C-Hour, Queens and UNGA81 documents. Their unchanged originals are available below, alongside reading guides to ten earlier papers.
    22. Sources & connectionsFind the projects that shaped these ideas, along with official and scientific sources for checking factual claims. Related projects explain the proposal; they do not independently prove it will work.
    23. About Mutual FuturesMutual Futures is Luke Nathan Hayes’s proposal for global systems change during the AI and automation transition, guided by Joyful Responsible Abundance. It connects productive ownership with income, learning, care, personal agency and time for life.
