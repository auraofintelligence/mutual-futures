Mutual Futures / Mutual wealth

02 / Ownership and stewardship

Own the future.
Together, by choice.

Mutual wealth has substance when the people involved understand what they own, how decisions are made, who carries risk and what remains for those who come next.

01 / EXPLORE

Three connected functions.

The architecture distinguishes local trading businesses, shared succession and investment capacity, and a sustainable employment and training function. They can cooperate without becoming one institution that owns a person's employer, health records, identity and civic choices.

01

Operating businesses

Continue serving customers through capable local management, with clearly defined member participation.

02

Shared investment capacity

Assess acquisitions, arrange capital, retain reserves and disclose charges, responsibilities and conflicts.

03

ready SET Co-op

The proposed ready sustainable employment and training cooperative connects paid learning, placements, shared equipment and transition support.

02 / EXPLORE

A mutual is more than a future promise.

Queensland guidance distinguishes distributing co-operatives, which can return eligible surplus to members, from non-distributing co-operatives, which do not distribute profits to members. Its distributing model uses one member, one vote. The legal form for this proposal remains a design decision rather than a registration claim.

03 / EXPLORE

The ownership questions stay visible.

QuestionWhat a public design would clarify
MembershipHow workers, customers, local businesses and communities relate to the assets.
Current benefitsHow wages, eligible distributions, services and shared facilities are funded.
Future membersWhich reserves and assets remain collective rather than being exhausted by the current cohort.
Moving or restingWhat happens to membership and accrued interests during a placement or agreed sabbatical.
LeavingHow any individual financial interest is valued and settled without pretending all shared reserves are withdrawable.
Decision-makingMember authority, delegated management, review, conflicts and amendments. No founder veto or other veto power is proposed.
04 / EXPLORE

Connection without ingestion.

First Nations retain authority over their own participation and knowledge. Individuals retain their private Aura and the permissions they choose. Commercial ownership does not amount to ownership of people, cultures or communities. Shared services and intellectual-property licences would have transparent terms rather than unexplained permanent dependency.

The federation can own assets. It does not own the lives built around them.
